She locked the phone, not the draft, and watched the terminal come up out of the morning, ordinary, busy, full of people whose day had not been asked to carry a number. The restraint was in force, or would be, at 15:00 UTC, which was soon and not the same as always. Whether it would still be in force when the date on the screen arrived was a question the document had declined to answer for her. She had signed that decline on purpose. Sitting with it, in a car, with an unsent line and an undeleted one, was the work the signature had bought. She did not know, yet, if they would do the work. The not-knowing was not a mood. It was the size of the thing. The terminal doors opened. She went through them with the draft still in the phone, live as a scratch buffer, honest for now, and not guaranteed to stay that way.
